## 3. Character & Artwork Coverage

Every character name, likeness, and piece of artwork introduced within this repository's game folders (`games/<game-name>/` and their associated asset paths) is an original creative work, conceived, directed, and owned exclusively by the Owner. This applies collectively and automatically to every game the Game Loader hosts, present and future, without requiring a named entry for each one.

Character artwork produced under this repository consistently carries the 3C brand's established colour identity and, in most cases, the 3C mark directly within the imagery itself, situating each character as brand-originated rather than a standalone or generic asset.

Any AI tools, design platforms, or production services used in the creation of a character's name, voice, artwork, or animation operate strictly as production instruments under the Owner's direct creative authority for that specific game, in the same manner already established in Section 6 of the parent declaration. No such tool or provider holds any claim of origination, co-authorship, or ownership over any character or artwork produced through this repository. Production credits for individual games, which tools were used for what, remain documented within that game's own credits, not within this legal record, since credits are production detail and will vary game to game.

## 4. How Protection Applies to Each Game

This document does not need to be updated each time a new character or game is added. Protection for a given game's characters and artwork is anchored to the point that game's files are first committed to this repository, verifiable independently via GitHub's own commit history for that folder path, in the same way Section 1 anchors the whole repository.

In practice: the moment a new game folder and its character assets are committed under `games/`, that character and its artwork fall under this declaration and the repository's Legal Disclaimer, timestamped by that commit, without any further action required here.
